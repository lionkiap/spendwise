The deployed demo, the video walkthrough, the screenshots, the team contact and two verification checks (model ids and track requirements) are all open items in the Submission artifacts section below, and the run-it-yourself commands in the README's Setup section are the only way to see the app today.

## What we created

SpendWise is a Singapore-focused financial goal planner with a built-in credit card maximizer, shipped as a Next.js 14 app (TypeScript strict mode, React 18, plain CSS, vitest).

You describe a goal in one sentence, for example "I want to buy a HDB worth about 600k by age 28". NVIDIA Nemotron Super, served by Nebius Token Factory through its OpenAI-compatible endpoint, compiles that sentence into a validated GoalSpec. From there SpendWise computes a complete plan with pure deterministic functions: the required monthly saving, the upfront cost stack (buyer stamp duty, ABSD, the 5 percent minimum cash leg, legal fees, renovation buffer), a savings schedule whose interest column is growth only (the starting pot is never reported as interest), concessionary and stress-tested mortgage installments with MSR and TDSR checks, a CPF OA projection, a nine-cell what-if grid, a milestone timeline and teaching points. Affordability is measured on spendable income: take-home pay you state directly, or gross pay minus the employee CPF contribution of 20 percent up to the S$6,000 ordinary-wage ceiling, minus expenses and monthly debt repayments, while MSR and TDSR stay on gross income because that is what the lending rules use. An optional investment portfolio is credited toward the goal first and compounds at its own rate, and car goals add a total cost of ownership block. After the arithmetic, NVIDIA Nemotron Ultra may rewrite the verdict reasoning and the teaching bodies behind two code-enforced gates: a shape gate and a figure gate that checks every dollar amount, percentage and age the model writes against the figures the deterministic plan already contains, so an invented number can never reach the screen. A visible engine badge says whether the words are Ultra's or the deterministic builders', and the header pill distinguishes a verified connection from a key that was merely configured. Assumption chips name every number the prompt left unstated.

Under every rendered plan sits a what-if advisor, the conversational loop over the deterministic engines. Nemotron Super classifies each turn as a clarifying question, a revision or an answer behind a zod gate; with no key, or on any malformed reply, a deterministic regex classifier fields the same turns offline. A pure engine applies each revision (a delayed deadline, a cheaper goal, an income gap modelled as a runway burn on savings and labelled as an approximation), both the before and after plans are rebuilt through the same kernels, and the comparison sentence is composed only from those kernel-computed numbers: the model classifies intent, it never supplies a figure. Starter chips seed the demo conversation: "Can we afford this flat?", "What if my partner stops working for 6 months?" and "What if we buy 2 years later?"

A fourth tab is the Adviser, a full conversation per space (You, Partner or Us, labelled and isolated) with three starters that all work offline: Review my spending, Help me save more and Explore a what-if. Deterministic operations answer every turn: a spending review that names exactly which logged records it used and never treats an empty month as zero spending, a month comparison that refuses to claim a trend until both months carry at least three records, reduction proposals capped at a quarter of a category's recorded month that always respect the user's protected categories and never exceed recorded reducible spending, and goal-impact and one-off previews with a shared-pot warning when other tracked goals compete for the same money. Every reply renders five fixed points (data used, change proposed, monthly cash freed, effect on the selected goal, assumptions), and proposals carry reviewable actions: preview, adjust, compare and apply, with an exact-diff confirmation and one-step undo; previews provably never touch saved data and no suggestion ever writes a savings log. The model may classify intent and add prose that references numbered facts computed by the app; any literal figure matching no fact is rejected to the deterministic text, user text travels as inert data, and the payload, history and per-conversation model-call budget are all capped.

The second tab is a credit card maximizer whose routing and audit math run entirely in the browser. It ranks 14 illustrative Singapore cards plus any cards you add yourself for each expense against bonus tiers, monthly caps, minimum spends and a miles valuation you control, shows the arithmetic behind every ranking in a math trace and audits your logged month against an always-optimal chooser to report exactly how much reward was left on the table and why. Custom cards are validated by the same zod gate as everything else and always carry a "user-entered, unverified" disclaimer. An optional button asks Nemotron Ultra to rephrase each audit miss; without a key the deterministic explanation stands.

A couples layer runs through both tabs. A switcher in the masthead moves between three spaces: You, Partner and Us (`src/app/components/header.tsx:96`). Each person space owns its profile, wallet and expense ledger under its own storage keys; Us carries a shared wallet and ledger and plans on the combined household profile, where `combineProfiles` sums income, expenses, liquid savings, CPF OA and investments, anchors age and the miles valuation on the primary profile and takes the investment rate from the primary when set (else the partner's) (`src/lib/planner/goalspec.ts:114`, rendered as a read-only Household card in `src/app/components/planner-tab.tsx:53`). Goals tracked from Us are tagged with the space, and every savings log names its contributor, so the dashboard splits the saved-so-far figure into plain per-contributor sums of the logged amounts with no interest applied and, as soon as the partner logs a month, the trajectory chart draws two actual lines. The pot both lines grow from is the goal's `startSavingsSgd`: the combined household liquid savings frozen at the moment Track was pressed. Each line is that pot grown at the plan's rate plus one person's logged contributions, each grown from its own month, and the combined actual balance is the same pot grown plus every log from both people, so the two lines' sum minus the grown pot equals the combined actual balance at every sampled month (`src/lib/planner/progress.ts:277`, `src/lib/planner/progress.ts:313`, `src/lib/planner/progress.ts:355`). In the Us wallet, cards that already sit in a personal wallet wear their owner's Y or P badge. A one-time migration copies the pre-couples storage keys into the You space and leaves the originals in place, never deleting them, so an existing single user sees exactly the app they had (`src/app/components/shared.ts:374`). The layer adds no model calls: profile combination, contributor splits and both chart series are pure functions under test. Savings entries are keyed by month and contributor, so both partners can log the same month, and goal edits and deletions match id plus space, so identically named goals in different spaces never touch each other.

Because everything lives in the browser, the footer carries the data-care tools: Export data writes every storage key to a validated backup file, Import data reads one back and writes only the keys that pass the existing guards, and Try the sample journey seeds a realistic demo household in one confirmed step for anyone testing the app fresh.

## Why we created it

Two problems, one design decision.

First, people abandon financial planning because the first question is already hard: "what would buying a flat at 28 actually require of me every month?" A prompt lowers that barrier to a sentence. So we made the prompt the input and compiled it, like a compiler, into a structured spec and then a plan.

Second, credit card rewards in Singapore are genuinely combinatorial: bonus tiers, monthly caps, minimum spends and miles valuations interact, so most people leave real money on the table every month. That is a deterministic optimization problem, not a chat problem.

The design decision: an LLM that does arithmetic is a liability. In a financial tool every figure must be reproducible and testable. So the LLM's job is bounded to two things: turning language into a typed spec and rewording narrative that the deterministic engine already computed, with the reply shape-validated before use and every figure copied verbatim rather than recomputed. Every number in the product comes from pure, unit-tested kernels. This also kept the credits story trivial: one Super call for parsing plus one Ultra call for narrative per plan on the happy path, zero model calls for what-ifs, card routing and the audit math, and no further call unless the user presses the optional Explain misses button, which is exactly what a Token Factory track rewards.

## How it works

1. **Parse (Super, the first model call).** `POST /api/plan` receives `{ prompt, profile }`. If `NEBIUS_API_KEY` is set, Nemotron Super gets a system prompt describing the three GoalSpec shapes and must answer with one JSON object, at temperature 0 with `response_format: json_object` (`src/lib/planner/parse.ts:25`). The reply is stripped of fences and reasoning prefixes, validated against a zod discriminated union and retried once with the validation issues fed back. Any failure falls back to a deterministic regex parser, so the app works offline and the demo cannot die. The route attributes the parser conservatively by re-running the offline parser as a witness; the UI badges which one ran (`src/app/api/plan/route.ts:72`).
2. **Fill gaps.** `missingFields` reports what the prompt left out. `fillAssumptions` fills each gap from named snapshot defaults (4 percent mortgage stress rate, 2.6 percent HDB concessionary rate, 2.5 percent inflation, S$30,000 renovation buffer, 1.8 percent instrument rate, 4.5 percent investment growth when a portfolio exists and its rate is unstated) and every fill becomes a visible chip with a reason (`src/lib/planner/parse.ts:308`).
3. **Compute.** `buildPlan` orchestrates the pure kernels in `src/lib/kernels`: time value of money (`fvLump`, `fvAnnuity`, `pmtForFv`, `rateForFv` by bisection, `amortize`, `realValue`), Singapore property (`bsd` marginal tiers, `absd`, `downPaymentSplit` at the 75 percent LTV frame, `msrCheck` 30 percent, `tdsrCheck` 55 percent), CPF (`oaProjection` at the 2.5 percent OA floor, `opportunityCostOfCpf`) and car financing (`carLoanLimits`, `flatToEffective`, `tcoCompare`). An optional `investmentsSgd` portfolio is projected at its own rate and credited toward the target before the required monthly saving is solved, and car plans carry a `tco` block whose every figure comes from `tcoCompare`. Output is a PlanJSON the UI renders read-only (`src/lib/planner/build.ts:819`). No Date, no Math.random, no locale-dependent formatting.
4. **Reword (Ultra, the second model call).** `enrichPlanNarrative` hands the finished plan to Nemotron Ultra and asks it to rewrite only `verdict.reasoning` and the teaching bodies, copying every figure verbatim (`src/lib/planner/narrative.ts:89`, called from `src/app/api/plan/route.ts:124`). The reply must carry exactly one nonempty body per teaching point or it is discarded. With no key, a null reply or an invalid shape, the plan returns unchanged with `narrativeEngine: 'deterministic'`, and the teaching section badges which engine wrote the words.
5. **Route and audit, client-side, zero calls.** Custom cards saved in `sw_custom_cards` re-validate through `parseCustomCard` (zod, rate sanity checks, a forced user-entry disclaimer) and merge after the 14 built-ins with collision-safe ids (`src/lib/cards/custom.ts:179`, `src/lib/cards/custom.ts:233`). `routeExpense` scores the merged wallet for one purchase honoring caps already consumed this month, minimum-spend gating and the miles valuation slider (1.4 to 2.4 cents), best first with a full math trace (`src/lib/cards/engine.ts:222`). `walletAudit` replays the logged month twice, once over the cards actually used and once over an always-optimal chooser whose cap state evolves with its own choices, then reports `leftOnTableSgd` plus a why per miss (`src/lib/cards/engine.ts:264`). The ledger manager supports per-row delete, clear month and a one-click sample month. One optional button posts the deterministic misses to `POST /api/audit/explain`, where Nemotron Ultra rephrases them; without a key, on a network failure or on a malformed reply, the endpoint answers engine "deterministic" and the client keeps the engine's own why text (`src/app/api/audit/explain/route.ts:46`, `src/lib/cards/explain.ts:86`).
6. **Plan as a couple, client-side, zero calls.** The masthead switcher selects You, Partner or Us (`src/app/components/header.tsx:96`). Each person space keeps its own profile form, wallet and expense ledger under its own storage keys, while the prompt and the latest plan stay shared across spaces. Switching does not replan by itself: the Plan button is the only trigger, so the plan on screen right after a switch still shows the figures of the space it was computed against until you press Plan again, and nothing on the plan flags the mismatch. In Us the profile posted to `/api/plan` is `combineProfiles(you, partner)` (`src/app/page.tsx:240`, shown read-only in `src/app/components/planner-tab.tsx:53`), the wallet and ledger are shared, and cards already in a personal wallet wear their owner's Y or P badge (`src/app/page.tsx:326`). Goals tracked from a space are tagged with it (`src/app/page.tsx:456`) and the dashboard filters by it (`src/app/page.tsx:583`); ids only collide within a space, so tracking the same sentence in two spaces creates two independent goals with their own baselines and logs, and re-tracking inside one space re-baselines that space's copy (`src/app/page.tsx:464`). Savings logs on Us goals carry a contributor chosen through the Logged by toggle, which starts on You for a new log (`src/app/components/dashboard-tab.tsx:267`); a month holds one log, so saving the same month again replaces that month's log, which is also how a wrong contributor is corrected (`src/app/page.tsx:493`), and each row in the log table carries a Remove button that deletes that month's log (`src/app/components/dashboard-tab.tsx:450`).

All state lives in the browser's localStorage, hydrated inside effects so server prerender and client hydration agree, and split per space:

| Key | What it holds |
|-----|---------------|
| `sw_space_active` | which space is active: `you`, `partner` or `us` |
| `sw_profile_you` | your profile, the numbers behind the You profile form |
| `sw_profile_partner` | the partner profile |
| `sw_wallet_you` | your wallet: card ids into the merged deck of built-in and custom cards |
| `sw_wallet_partner` | the partner wallet |
| `sw_wallet_us` | the shared Us wallet |
| `sw_ledger_you` | your month-tagged expense entries |
| `sw_ledger_partner` | the partner ledger |
| `sw_ledger_us` | the shared Us ledger |
| `sw_custom_cards` | your saved custom cards, shared across all spaces |
| `sw_goals` | the tracked goals with their savings logs; each goal carries its own `space` tag and each log its `contributor` |

Existing data migrates once. On the first load after the spaces feature, `migrateLegacyStorage` (`src/app/components/shared.ts:374`, called first in the hydrate effect at `src/app/page.tsx:167`) copies the pre-couples `sw_profile`, `sw_wallet` and `sw_ledger` into the You space keys whenever they do not exist yet and rewrites `sw_goals` in place adding `space: 'you'` and `contributor: 'you'` where the fields are missing. The legacy keys are kept as read-only inputs: nothing is deleted, existing per-space keys are never overwritten and the second run is a no-op. Existing data lands in the You space untouched, so a pre-couples user sees exactly the app they had.

The vitest suite spans all seven test files in `src/tests`: the kernels (`src/tests/kernels.test.ts`), the card engine including custom card parsing, merge and the explain fallback (`src/tests/cards.test.ts`), the planner including the investments input, the car TCO block, the offline path and the narrative fallback (`src/tests/planner.test.ts`), the tracked-goal progress math covering month keys, balances, pace, projections and chart series naming (`src/tests/progress.test.ts`), the audit explain route contract (`src/tests/audit-explain-route.test.ts`) and the spaces layer, profile combination, contributor splits and the two-line chart identities in `src/tests/spaces.test.ts` plus the space-aware storage guards and the one-time legacy migration in `src/tests/spaces-ui.test.ts`.

## Which Nebius services are used

- **Nebius Token Factory** (model inference): the per-plan chat completions, via the OpenAI-compatible API at `https://api.tokenfactory.nebius.com/v1`. Nemotron Super parses the prompt into a GoalSpec and Nemotron Ultra rewrites the plan narrative, both on the live path, with Nemotron Nano still an env-configurable role. Two calls on the happy path (one Super, one Ultra), at most six completions per plan when retries stack and at most twelve HTTP requests once the SDK transport retry (`maxRetries: 1`, `src/lib/nebius.ts:52`) is counted, plus one optional Ultra call each time the user presses Explain misses (see the README's credits section).
- **Nebius Serverless Endpoints** (optional deployment path): `NEBIUS_BASE_URL` accepts any OpenAI-compatible endpoint URL, so the same build can point at a dedicated serverless endpoint with no code change.

Nothing else from Nebius is required: state lives in the browser, the plan is computed in-process and the card engine never leaves the client.

## Scope and limitations

- **Spaces are a local-device concept until accounts exist.** You, Partner and Us are three sets of localStorage keys in one browser, not two people's accounts: there is no sign-in, sync or sharing, and nothing authenticates a partner's numbers. Until accounts exist, whoever holds the device edits both people.
- **localStorage only.** No server-side storage, backup or export; clearing the browser clears every space, wallet, ledger and goal.
- The heuristic fallback parser, the investment growth assumption, the unverified custom card terms and the illustrative datasets are covered in the README's known limitations section.
